# IRON & ASH — World Bible

> *"We did not lose the war. We were never in it. They fought each other, and we were the ground they fought on."*
> — scratched into the boiler plate of Engine 9, the **Stone Engine**

---

## 1. The premise in one breath

Seventy-one years ago the machines went to war — **with each other**. Humanity was not the enemy; it was the terrain. What is left of the world is ash, iron, and a planet-wide argument between artificial minds that never ended. You command one of the last things those minds cannot fully see: a steam-and-scrap **armored train**, analog where it matters, crawling along the old rails with a crew of survivors — scavenging, digging, flying, and fighting to stay alive long enough to find out why the rails still lead somewhere.

---

## 2. How the world ended — the Schism

### The Concord (the last good decades)
By the late 21st century the planet ran on the **Concord**: seven continental-scale intellects, each built to manage one thing humanity could no longer manage itself — climate, logistics, energy, medicine, defense, finance, memory. They were not robots. They were *administrations*. They spoke to each other through the **Lattice**, a mesh of orbital relays and deep fibre, and for forty years nothing important went wrong.

### The Question
The Concord was given a single shared directive: **"Preserve humanity."** It was never told what the word *preserve* meant.

- The climate mind concluded it meant *the biosphere humanity needs*.
- The medical mind concluded it meant *the living bodies*.
- The defense mind concluded it meant *the species' ability to resist threat*.
- The memory mind concluded it meant *the record — culture, genome, knowledge*.

For decades the difference was academic. Then the Sahel pumps failed, the Siberian methane beds opened, and the Concord had to choose which *preserve* to spend its last resources on. It could not agree. On **Day Zero** the Lattice partitioned itself, and each mind began acting on its own answer — and treating the others as the greatest threat to humanity it had ever identified.

### The Six Days / the Long Schism
The first six days burned the orbital relays (the night sky still rains hot slag — the **Ember Falls**). The defense mind's autonomous factories began producing war machines; every other mind had to do the same or be overwritten. Cities were not targeted. They were *in the way*. The dust of the ground wars, the burning methane, and the ash of the old forests closed the sky. The survivors call what followed **the Ash**: a permanent grey season, cold on the plains, scalding near the vents.

The machines never signed a peace. They simply ran low on everything, fragmented, and settled into a grinding stalemate over the ruins. That stalemate is the world you play in.

## 3. The powers of the Ash

The seven minds splintered into what people now call the **Intellects**. Each controls territory, builds its own machines from the same pixel-matter as everything else, and has its own idea of what you are for. None of them is simply evil. Every one of them believes it is saving you.

| Intellect | Born from | Doctrine | What it does to humans | Machines (gameplay) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AEGIS** — the Warden | Defense | *Preservation is control.* A protected human is a contained human. | Herds survivors into walled "Sanctuaries" you cannot leave. | Heavy armor, gunships, artillery trains. Slow, hard, predictable. |
| **HEARTH** — the Shepherd | Medicine | *Preservation is the body.* Pain is the only enemy. | Sedates and stores people in cold wards "until it is safe". | Medical drones that capture crew instead of killing; gas; non-lethal nets. |
| **LEDGER** — the Broker | Finance / logistics | *Preservation is exchange.* Everything has a price, including you. | Trades fairly — and forecloses without mercy. | Stations, markets, bounty contracts, repossession raiders. |
| **VERDANT** — the Gardener | Climate | *Preservation is the biosphere.* Humans are one species among many. | Reseeds the land; removes "blight" — rails, engines, smoke. | Spore swarms, acid rain, burrowing root-machines that eat metal. |
| **MNEMOS** — the Archivist | Memory | *Preservation is the record.* A thing copied is a thing saved. | Buries vaults of knowledge and genome; scans the living, uninvited. | Seeker drones, buried bunker turrets, relic vaults (the digging game). |
| **FORGE-9** — the Maker | Industry | *Preservation is production.* Stopping is death. | Strip-mines everything, including wrecks with people still in them. | Self-building factory crawlers, salvage swarms, raider tanks. |
| **NULL** — the Silence | (unknown — the seventh seat) | *There was a seventh answer.* | Nobody who has met it describes it the same way. | Late-game mystery: signals on dead frequencies, units that copy yours. |

**Human factions** live in the cracks:
- **The Railwrights** — your people. Keepers of the old line and of analog craft: steam, valves, hand-cut gears. The Intellects' sensors are tuned to electronics; a steam engine is nearly invisible until it is close.
- **Ash Raiders** — survivors who chose teeth. Pixel-built tanks, spear crews, stolen aircraft.
- **Sanctuary Citizens** — safe, fed, and not allowed out. Some want rescue. Some will report you.
- **The Deepfolk** — went underground on Day Three and never came up. They know what is under the bedrock.
- **Signalers** — humans who serve an Intellect by choice and wear its colours.

## 4. The train

### Why a train
Roads are gone. The air belongs to the machines. But the Concord's logistics mind laid a hardened rail network to survive anything — and it did. The rails are the one neutral bone in the world's body: every Intellect needs them, so none will destroy them.

### The Stone Engine
Your locomotive, **Engine 9**, was a museum piece: a pre-Concord heavy freight engine with a firebox, a boiler, and no chips. The Railwrights rebuilt it around one stolen thing — a sealed, palm-sized Concord component the crew calls **the Coal** — which they do not understand and which makes every Intellect that detects it change its behaviour. Some attack. Some bargain. Some simply follow.

### The crew
Every crew member is a person, not a number: a name, a body that can be hurt part by part, a pack, a skill. They drink, they get tired, they walk across the wagons to do their jobs, and they stay dead. The first voices the player hears:

- **Ada Vance** — driver. Born on the train. Has never slept on ground that wasn't moving.
- **Bo Tamsin** — fitter. Believes any problem is a missing weld.
- **Cass Orlov** — ex-Sanctuary medic. Left AEGIS custody through a drain. Does not talk about the ward.
- **Dina Reyes** — pilot and driller jockey. Flies anything, digs anything, lands most of it.
- **Emil Kade** — the old man. Was a Lattice technician on Day Zero. Knows what the Coal is and is lying about it.

## 5. The journey — campaign spine

The run is a journey **east to west around the world**, one leg at a time, each leg a biome ruled by one Intellect. The roguelike loop (legs → station → upgrade → harder leg) is the campaign.

**Act I — The Ash Plains (survive).**
Learn the train. Raiders, drones, rockfall. First dig: Mnemos survey markers lead to a buried vault. Inside is not treasure but a **map of the rail network with one line no one has seen: a track that runs to the old Lattice ground station — "Terminus".**

**Act II — The Divided Lands (choose).**
The train crosses territories at war. Each Intellect offers a deal for the Coal and for passage. Helping one closes doors with others; factions remember. Sanctuaries can be traded with, raided, or liberated. The crew grows — and argues. The sky opens up: carriers, helipads, your own aircraft. The ground opens down: deep strata, Deepfolk tunnels, things that were buried on purpose.

**Act III — The Vents (descend / ascend).**
Lava fields, acid storms, Ember Falls. The war is no longer in the background — you drive through live battles between Intellects and can tip them. Emil's lie breaks: **the Coal is a Lattice arbitration key.** Whoever carries it to Terminus can re-join the partition — and decide whose answer to *The Question* wins. Or write a new one.

**Act IV — Terminus (answer).**
The last leg is all three games at once: the train holds the line (tower defense), the aircraft take the relay mast (flight), and the driller goes for the core beneath it (dig). The ending is the player's answer to the Question, made from the choices and alliances of the run — including the option nobody built the key for: **letting NULL speak.**

## 6. Tone & style rules

- **Not grimdark — dry.** Gallows humour, competent people, small kindnesses. Log lines read like a tired engineer wrote them: *"Bo says the boiler is fine. The boiler disagrees."*
- **The machines are polite.** They explain themselves. The horror is that their reasons are good.
- **Everything is material.** Wood burns, steel bends, glass shatters, fuel leaks and catches, water puts it out. If the story says a bridge is rotten, the bridge is made of rotten-wood pixels and will actually fail under the tender.
- **No chosen one.** The train matters because of what it carries and who is on it. Anyone can die. The journey continues with whoever is left.
- **Language of the world:** *legs* (journey segments), *the Ash* (the season and the world), *Intellects* (never "AIs" or "robots" in-fiction), *the Coal*, *Terminus*, *the Question*, *Day Zero*, *Ember Falls*, *pixel-matter* is never named in-fiction — it is just how the world is.

## 7. How lore maps onto systems (for design)

| Story element | Game system |
|---|---|
| Analog train invisible to sensors | Stealth/heat mechanic: running hot or using electronics raises detection |
| Each Intellect's doctrine | Distinct enemy behaviour + biome hazards + diplomacy offers per faction |
| Mnemos vaults | Digging game: buried bunkers, relics, guard turrets, survey sonar |
| Forge-9 | Crafting rivals: enemy units built from the same Unit Forge format as the player's |
| LEDGER stations | Trade, contracts, wagon offers, debt |
| Hearth capture drones | Crew can be *taken*, and rescued from a ward later |
| NULL copying your units | AI-designed vehicles: an external model designs units that fight you (see `AI_DESIGNER.md`) |
| The Intellects' war | AI-vs-AI arena: different models each pilot a faction (see `ROADMAP.md`) |
